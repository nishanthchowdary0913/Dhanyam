A clear weekly story of the work we did, the practices we observed, and the lessons we are sharing with farmers.

The photographs below show field visits, crop observation, farmer interaction, natural manure, irrigation, and project documentation.

WEEK 01

Understanding the field

  1. Received permission to conduct the community farming study.
  2. Surveyed farmers about natural and conventional paddy practices.
  3. Visited fields to observe crop growth and plant condition.
  4. Compared buffalo-dung inputs with chemical pesticides.
  5. Collected photographs and initial field observations.
WEEK 02

Comparing farming methods

  1. Surveyed paddy fields to understand local methods.
  2. Observed fields where chemical pesticides were used.
  3. Studied dried buffalo dung as a natural input.
  4. Monitored plant growth after different applications.
  5. Compared natural and chemical-based fields.
WEEK 03

Learning from observation

  1. Collected information about local paddy cultivation.
  2. Observed crop condition in chemical-based fields.
  3. Studied buffalo dung and its effect on soil fertility.
  4. Recorded how regular observation identifies crop problems.
  5. Compared effects on crop growth and the environment.
WEEK 04

Managing water wisely

  1. Observed irrigation methods and water channels.
  2. Studied how water levels influence crop growth.
  3. Discussed drainage and removal of excess water.
  4. Compared water management in different fields.
  5. Learned to adjust irrigation to soil, crop, and weather.
WEEK 05

Building soil health

  1. Continued field visits and farmer discussions.
  2. Compared chemical pesticides with natural manure.
  3. Studied buffalo dung as a source of crop nutrients.
  4. Observed plant height, leaf colour, soil, and water.
  5. Connected natural inputs with soil health and productivity.
WEEK 06

Documenting the learning

  1. Surveyed farmers about pesticides used in paddy cultivation.
  2. Collected information about pest management practices.
  3. Studied how buffalo dung is obtained and prepared.
  4. Recorded farmer methods for manure use and crop care.
  5. Organised evidence for the project comparison.
WEEK 07

Production and profit

  1. Estimate expected paddy production from the field area.
  2. Record the expected selling value of organic paddy.
  3. List input, labour, irrigation, and cultivation costs.
  4. Compare expected income with total cost.
  5. Present the possible profit and sustainable value.
WEEK 08

Presenting to farmers

  1. Present the Dhanyam website as a learning resource.
  2. Explain the organic paddy practices studied.
  3. Show the natural-input and chemical-input comparison.
  4. Share the production and profit representation.
  5. Discuss practical improvements with multiple farmers.
